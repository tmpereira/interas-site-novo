# Atrial Fibrillation and Inflammation: Prof. Emiliano Medei's Discovery that Made the Cover of Nature

Atrial Fibrillation (AF) is the most common cardiac arrhythmia in clinical practice, significantly increasing the risk of stroke and heart failure. But what really causes this "mismatch" in the heart's atrium? [Prof. Emiliano Medei](/equipe/emiliano-horacio-medei/)'s research group has published a study of global impact that brings an innovative answer: the culprit may be our defense cells.

![](/noticias/2025-04-02-prof-emiliano-artigo-capa-nature/3.png)

## The Invisible Villain: Interleukin-1β

Scientists already knew that patients with high levels of an inflammatory protein called IL-1β were more likely to develop arrhythmias. Prof. Emiliano's study proved this connection directly: continuous exposure to this protein creates a vicious cycle of inflammation that "scars" (fibrosis) the heart tissue and alters its electrical properties.

## The Surprise: The Heart Is Not the Only Culprit

The big turning point of this research was identifying what responds to the inflammation. Using cutting-edge technology, the team showed that the problem does not start directly in the heart's muscle cells (cardiomyocytes), but in resident macrophages — immune system cells that live inside the heart.

* The Mechanism: The IL-1β protein activates a receptor on these macrophages, which then produce even more inflammation.
* The Short Circuit: This process shortens the electrical recovery time of the left atrium, making the heart "irritable" and prone to fibrillation.

## Treatments of the Future

By specifically deactivating the inflammation receptor on these macrophages, the researchers were able to prevent the arrhythmia. This changes everything! Instead of just treating the electrical symptom with common antiarrhythmics, science now looks to therapies that interrupt the inflammatory cycle at its source.

> "These findings offer a new therapeutic perspective: targeting IL-1β signaling may be the key to treating patients with Atrial Fibrillation who do not respond well to conventional treatments," the research highlights.

For INTERAS, sharing this milestone — which deservedly made the cover of one of the world's leading scientific journals — reinforces the importance of excellent basic research for building a more precise and humane medicine.
