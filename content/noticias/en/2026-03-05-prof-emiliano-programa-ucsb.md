Prof. Emiliano, vice-coordinator of **INTERAS (INCT for Radiation in Health)**, has been selected to take part in the international program *"Multi-Scale Physics of Normal and Diseased Heart: from Ion Channels to Whole Organ"*, to be held at the renowned **Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics (KITP)**, University of California, Santa Barbara (UCSB).

Prof. Emiliano's selection is a significant milestone, since the program brings together a select group of researchers from around the world, with few representatives from Latin America, for a 30-day immersion in one of the most complex frontiers of contemporary biophysics.

### The Challenge of Cardiac Arrhythmias
Although cardiac arrhythmia is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, its biological mechanisms are still not fully understood. The difficulty lies in the nature of the heart as a multiscale system: what happens at the molecular level (ion channels) reverberates through nonlinear interactions that affect the organ as a whole.

The UCSB program proposes a transdisciplinary approach, integrating clinical medicine, physics, mathematics and engineering to model these phenomena at different scales of time and space. The event is organized around four fundamental pillars:

1.  **Molecular Level:** Analysis of genetic and molecular bases.
2.  **Cellular Phenomena:** Study of calcium signaling and abnormal electrical excitations.
3.  **Tissue Level:** Investigation of reentry processes and ectopic excitations.
4.  **Clinical Application:** Development of new tools for diagnosis, prediction and treatment.

### Impact for INTERAS and Internationalization
For Prof. Emiliano, taking part is a unique opportunity for exchange with world-class centers of excellence. More than an individual achievement, the initiative aims to strengthen the role of INTERAS on the international scientific scene.

> "I hope to help broaden horizons within INCT-INTERAS, fostering international collaborations that allow us to advance not only in theoretical knowledge but also in the training of human resources," says the researcher.

This immersion is expected to open new fronts of cooperation for the graduate programs linked to INTERAS, giving young researchers and PhD students access to methodologies and networks that are essential for the advancement of Brazilian health science.
